# Research basis used for V9

## Warm-up
The V9 warm-ups are dynamic and task-specific: raise temperature, mobilize the relevant joints through usable ROM, activate the muscles that will be used, then move directly into the first skill/strength pattern. Long static stretching is not the priority immediately before explosive work. The NSCA dynamic warm-up guidance describes increases in blood flow/core temperature, joint ROM, nerve-impulse speed, and rate of force development as reasons to use a well-designed dynamic warm-up.

## Hypertrophy / calisthenics
Bodyweight training can build muscle without external weights, and progressive calisthenics can improve upper-body strength. The app therefore treats harder leverage/ROM variants, band resistance, and adequate proximity to failure as progression tools rather than relying on endless repetitions of an easy variation.

## Band policy
- Assistance bands: skill practice/volume, especially Front Touch, OAP, FL pull-up. Use the lightest band that preserves the intended pattern.
- Resistance bands: hypertrophy/volume once the bodyweight movement is comfortably within the programmed target.
- Warm-up bands: light, never fatiguing.

## Sunday legs
Power work is placed before fatigue-heavy strength work. The Sunday session uses broad jumps and countermovement jumps first, then Bulgarian split squats, pistols, unilateral hinge work, jump lunges, calves and band leg curls. The design intentionally separates high-quality explosive contacts from later hypertrophy work.

## EMOM logic
The app now treats each EMOM minute as a fixed 60-second window. Reps can be completed early (e.g. in 25–30 seconds), logged immediately, and the clock continues until the minute boundary. The next minute begins exactly when the previous 60-second window ends. This matches the user's intended use better than restarting a 60-second timer after the rep entry.

## Corrected oversimplifications
The app does not treat pump/burning as proof of fast-twitch fibre recruitment, does not require absolute failure on every set, and does not assume bodyweight work is automatically safer just because it has no external load. Those are too simplistic for programming an advanced athlete.
